Chapter VI

The Paradigm
Shift

Share of list entries by gender, 1931–2020

In the early decades, male voices completely dominated. The 1930s and '40s saw men holding over 70% of list spots, reflecting the literary establishment of the era.

By the late 1950s and into the '60s, a slow shift began. Female authors gained ground — still a minority, but consistently growing.

Then came the 1970s — the turning point. For the first time, female authorship reached and even briefly crossed the 50% mark in isolated years.

From the 2010s onward, female authorship never looked back. By 2020, women held 59.3% of all list entries — the highest ever recorded.

Epilogue

Selling Well
vs. Being Good?

Of 7,431 unique titles on the NYT list, roughly 15–16% were by Pulitzer Prize-winning authors — nearly equal between genders. And 91% of female Pulitzer Fiction winners appeared on the bestseller list. Literary prestige and commercial success are not opposites.
